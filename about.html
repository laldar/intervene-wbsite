About INTERVENE

The Research Problem

Tensions and conflicts between groups, ranging from prejudice toward minority groups and polarized political climates, to devastating wars, are pressing global problems. Thus, there is a wide need for evidence-based approaches to improve intergroup relations. As part of this global effort, social scientists have been developing and assessing psychological intergroup interventions, achieving huge theoretical and methodological advancements.

Nevertheless, a comprehensive theoretical modelling and assessment of how contexts impact conflict-related individual-level psychological variables, and in turn, how this shapes the effectiveness of interventions across the different contexts, is yet to be established, currently limiting the effectiveness and durability of applied interventions.

"[Psychological intergroup interventions are] based on psychological theory that deliberately attempt to alter attitudes, emotions, perceived norms, or behavior that constitute barriers to — or that can facilitate the promotion of — tolerant, peaceful, and equal relations between members of different social groups."

Halperin, Hameiri & Littman, 2023
The Four Research Aims

INTERVENE models conflict context — level of violence, perceived threat, and the (a)symmetry of power between groups — as the driver of the psychological cognitive structure interventions actually work on: attitudes, beliefs, emotions, meta-perceptions, and perceived norms, which constitute the guiding logic for interventions.

01

Develop a comprehensive theoretical framework for the impact of the contexts of intergroup conflicts on individual-level, intergroup-related psychological variables, using a large-scale, multi-cultural global study.

02

Compare data-driven approaches to identify the specific psychological variables — e.g., attitudes, perceived norms — that interventions should target.

03

Assess the effectiveness of interventions across contexts in online studies.

04

Test real-world interventions in large-scale and longitudinal field studies.

The First Step: A Large-Scale, Multi-Cultural Global Study

To increase the external validity of the model, this project phase represents each type of prototypical conflictual relations — prejudice, political polarization, and escalating and de-escalating violent intergroup conflict — with at least three specific contexts.

As a next step, INTERVENE will test interventions directly against each other across contexts: four intervention tournaments, run in parallel across diverse settings, will pit interventions that target different psychological variables against one another to find out which hold up best. The winning interventions will then move into a large-scale, longitudinal field study in live conflict settings.

Testing Whether the Model Generalizes

Beyond WEIRD countries. Since the vast majority of social psychological research is conducted in WEIRD countries (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic), this project phase is designed to examine whether the model generalizes to non-WEIRD countries as well — a deliberate part of the twelve-context design, not an afterthought.

Sample size. For accurate estimations in multilevel analysis, at least 200 participants are collected from each adversarial group within each conflictual context — a total of 7,200 participants across the twelve core contexts.

Local research partners. INTERVENE draws on established partners from our existing network, alongside additional collaborators interested in joining, to build a rich and diverse dataset from around the world.
